# 14. Income & tax deductions

Estimate the tax return's "income deductions" and "tax calculation" sections on the "Deductions" screen.

**Language**: [日本語](14-income-deductions.md) | **English** | [繁體中文](14-income-deductions_zh-TW.md)

> **By the end of this chapter you can**
> - Understand what aoiko covers (deduction/credit estimates on top of business income) and what it doesn't (items with complex individual circumstances)
> - Enter family, insurance, medical expense, and donation data and see the estimate from the basic deduction through to the special reconstruction income tax
> - Know how to reflect income deductions/credits in the `.xtx` export
>
> **Prerequisite**: business income (the PL side of the financial statements) is finalized. [06. Reports](06-reports_en.md).

## 1. What aoiko covers

aoiko is fundamentally a "business P&L only" tool, but at the user's request it now also estimates the tax return's (KOA020, page 1) "income deductions" and "tax calculation" sections (below).

| Category | Contents |
|---|---|
| Other income | Salary income (the deduction is computed from Income Tax Act Art. 28(4)'s Schedule 5 when salary income is under ¥6.6M, or from the statutory table above that); miscellaneous income (public pension is entered as a final amount, other misc. income is revenue minus expenses — under the Sochiho Art. 27 home-worker special provision below (§ 4), expenses are the post-provision amount) |
| Real estate income (only if enabled in Settings) | Profit/loss is derived automatically from journal entries (recorded with the business/real estate toggle). Estimates the self-assessed business-scale status, the shared blue-return deduction pool with business income, and the loss-offsetting limitation from land-acquisition loan interest |
| Income deductions | Basic deduction, social insurance deduction, small business mutual aid deduction, life insurance deduction, earthquake insurance deduction, medical expense deduction, donation deduction, disability deduction, single parent/widow deduction, working student deduction, spouse deduction/special spouse deduction, dependent deduction, specific relative special deduction |
| Tax credits | Dividend tax credit, housing loan special tax credit, political donation special tax credit, housing seismic renovation special tax credit etc., foreign tax credit etc., other tax credits, disaster exemption amount |
| Tax calculation | Taxable income, income tax (progressive rate), special reconstruction income tax, estimated tax due/refund after subtracting withholding tax |

> **The casualty loss deduction, mortgage tax credit, and foreign tax credit are entered as final confirmed amounts.** Because they depend heavily on individual circumstances (disaster details, the year-end mortgage balance history, foreign tax payment certificates, etc.) and the rules are complex, aoiko doesn't calculate these — instead, transcribe the final amount from the NTA's worksheet or your accountant. Likewise, the "classification" codes for the spouse deduction, dependent deduction, specific relative special deduction, mortgage tax credit, and foreign tax credit (codes representing relationship or mortgage contract type) are not output, so complete them in e-Tax yourself if you use these deductions.
>
> **Public pension income is also entered as a final confirmed amount.** Its formula depends on age, pension amount, and other income across a frequently-revised bracket table, so aoiko doesn't calculate it — transcribe the final amount from the NTA's public pension income worksheet.
>
> **The payee breakdowns for rent paid, loan interest paid, and professional fees for real estate income (the detail pages of KOA220/KOA130) are also entered as final confirmed amounts.** The rental property details (property detail) are entered from the fixed asset registration in [08. Depreciation § 2-5](08-depreciation_en.md#2-5-fixed-assets-for-real-estate-income-only-if-enabled-in-settings).
>
> **Bad debt for real estate income (write-off vs. allowance) is recorded under two separate accounts.** "貸倒金（不動産）" (bad debt write-off) is an actual realized loss for a business-scale rental, deducted as a necessary expense in the year the loss arises (Income Tax Act art. 51(2)); "貸倒引当金繰入額（不動産）" (bad debt allowance) is a reserve provision (Income Tax Act art. 52-1, only for a business-scale rental). Real estate income can only use the individually-assessed allowance — the lump-sum allowance (art. 52-2) requires both a blue return and business income, so it doesn't apply here. The individually-assessed allowance has no blue-return requirement, so it's a deductible expense on a white return too as long as it's a business-scale rental. Whether it's business-scale is set by the self-assessment in the "Real estate income" row above; aoiko does not verify the actual facts (e.g. the "5 buildings or 10 units" rule of thumb). For a non-business-scale rental with a bad debt, it is not a necessary expense; under Income Tax Act art. 64(1), the portion of income corresponding to the uncollectible amount is treated as never having existed. If the year is still unfiled, simply reduce/reverse the corresponding rent journal entry directly; if it relates to an already-filed year, use the correction-entry flow in [12. Amended Return](12-amended_en.md) (there's no dedicated bad-debt feature — it routes through the existing correction flow).

## 2. Input fields

Enter data from the **"Deductions"** navigation item.

- **Other income** (optional): salary income (enter the amount paid and withholding tax from your withholding slip); miscellaneous income (public pension as a final amount, other misc. income as revenue and expenses); withholding tax on the business income side (final amount)
- **Real estate income** (only shown if enabled in Settings): whether it's a business-scale rental (self-assessed), interest on debt incurred to acquire land (final amount), payee breakdowns for rent paid / loan interest paid / professional fees (multiple entries per category)
- **Salary income** (shown for the 2026 tax year only): the "Last salary payment was before December 1, 2026" checkbox (switches between the old/new Schedule 5 table; unchecked = new table)
- **Additional blue-return deduction inputs**: "Gross business income two years prior", "Gross real estate income two years prior (business-scale only)". The cash-basis election (Income Tax Act Art. 67(1)) is set in the blue-return special deduction section of [01. Setup](01-setup_en.md)
- The "Apply the special necessary-expense rule for home workers, etc. (Special Taxation Measures Act Art. 27)" checkbox (see § 4 below)
- **Family**: whether you have a spouse and their total income/age; dependents (name, age, total income, co-residing lineal ascendant) registered per person
- **Social insurance / life insurance, etc.**: social insurance and small business mutual aid contributions paid; life insurance (by new/old system and general/medical-nursing-care/individual pension category); earthquake insurance and the transitional measure (old long-term casualty insurance)
- **Medical expenses / donations / casualty loss**: medical expenses paid and insurance reimbursement received; total donations (including furusato nozei); casualty loss deduction (final amount)
- **Your status**: whether you qualify as disabled, special disabled, single parent, widow, or working student
- **Tax credits**: dividend tax credit, mortgage tax credit, political donation special tax credit, housing seismic renovation special tax credit etc., foreign tax credit, other tax credits, disaster exemption amount (all entered as final amounts)

Input is saved per year (it isn't automatically carried over from the previous year).

## 3. Estimate results

The bottom of the screen shows each deduction amount, the total income deductions, taxable income, income tax after credits, the special reconstruction income tax, the final income tax + special reconstruction income tax amount, withholding tax, and the estimated tax due/refund. Business income is derived automatically from the financial statements' totals and can't be edited on this screen. Total income adds any salary/misc income entered under "Other income" and real estate income (the offsettable portion) to business income, and that combined figure is what drives the basic deduction bracket, etc. When the Sochiho Art. 27 home-worker special provision (§ 4) applies, the business income and miscellaneous income shown here won't match the financial statements' P&L (which is before the special provision).

> The basic deduction is the year-by-year bracketed amount under Income Tax Act art. 86 and the special provisions law art. 41-16-2, and varies by total income. For Reiwa 8/9 (tax years 2026/2027): ¥1,040,000 for total income ≤¥4.89M, ¥670,000 for ≤¥6.55M, ¥620,000 for ≤¥23.5M. For Reiwa 7 (2025): ¥950,000 for ≤¥1.32M, ¥880,000 for ≤¥3.36M, ¥680,000 for ≤¥4.89M, ¥630,000 for ≤¥6.55M, ¥580,000 for ≤¥23.5M. From Reiwa 10 (2028) onward: ¥990,000 for ≤¥1.32M, ¥620,000 for ≤¥23.5M. The taper above ¥23.5M (¥480,000/¥320,000/¥160,000) is the same in every year.
>
> Salary income is derived from Income Tax Act Art. 28(4)'s Schedule 5 below ¥6.6M in salary, and from the statutory table under Art. 28(3) at ¥6.6M and above. For the 2026/2027 tax years (Reiwa 8/9), the Special Provisions Law Art. 29-4(2) table takes priority over Schedule 5 in the range ¥691,000–¥2,200,000: the salary income amount is ¥0 for ¥691,000–¥741,000, income minus ¥740,000 for ¥741,000–¥2,191,000, ¥1,451,000 for ¥2,191,000–¥2,193,000, ¥1,453,000 for ¥2,193,000–¥2,196,000, and ¥1,456,000 for ¥2,196,000–¥2,200,000 (each band's lower bound is inclusive and upper bound exclusive). Income below ¥6.6M outside that range (under ¥691,000, and ¥2.2M up to ¥6.6M) uses Schedule 5. For the 2026 tax year, use Schedule 5's old table if the last salary payment was before December 1, 2026, or the new table otherwise (Suppl. Prov. Art. 13(2); e.g. ¥670,000 of income gives ¥20,000 of salary income under the old table, ¥0 under the new table). From the 2028 tax year (Reiwa 10) on, the Art. 29-4 special provision ends and ¥691,000–¥6.6M uses Schedule 5 (new table only). In every tax year, ¥6.6M and above uses the base rule under Income Tax Act Art. 28(3) (salary income deduction: 10% of income + ¥1,100,000 up to ¥8.5M, and ¥1,950,000 above ¥8.5M).
>
> Choose the blue-return deduction type in [01. Setup](01-setup_en.md). From the 2027 tax year (Reiwa 9) on, there are three tiers:
> - **¥650,000**: requires double-entry bookkeeping for a business running an enterprise generating business income or a business-scale real estate rental (a loss in business income doesn't change that you're "running" it; NTA Q&A case 「業務的規模の不動産所得と赤字の事業所得がある場合の青色申告特別控除」 (blue-return special deduction when there is non-business-scale real estate income and a loss-making business)), not electing the cash-basis method under Income Tax Act Art. 67(1), recording every transaction as the ministerial ordinance requires, and e-filing the return with the balance sheet and P/L by the deadline
> - **¥750,000**: on top of the ¥650,000 requirements, under Sochiho Ministerial Ordinance Art. 9-6(3)/(6), keeping and preserving the journal and general ledger as electronic records — consistently from the very first recording — under Electronic Books Storage Act Art. 4(1) or 5(1)/(3), plus meeting item 1 (the Art. 8(4) qualified-e-book requirements, notified under Storage Ministerial Ordinance Art. 5(1)) or item 2 (under Sochiho Ministerial Ordinance Art. 9-6(9)/(10): (イ) using a qualifying system to process electronic transactions in the course of business, and (ロ) preserving that year's electronic-transaction records within that system per Storage Ministerial Ordinance Art. 5(5); notified under Art. 5(6)), with either notification filed by the filing deadline (Sochiho Ministerial Ordinance Art. 9-6(5)/(8))
> - **¥100,000**: drops to ¥0 for a filer running a business (condition ①) not electing the cash-basis method (condition ②), using simple bookkeeping (condition ③, not falling under item 4), whose total revenue from that income (real estate business uses real estate, business uses business), two years prior, exceeded ¥10M (condition ④, the Para. 2 test; entered in § 2). This Para. 2 test only applies when the **originally chosen** type is simple bookkeeping — it has no effect for double-entry filers or anyone electing the cash-basis method
>
> For the 2026 tax year (Reiwa 8): you get ¥650,000 by meeting **either** item 1 (qualified e-books) **or** item 2 (on-time e-Tax submission) of the ¥650,000 requirements above — not both; double-entry bookkeeping meeting neither gets ¥550,000; anything else gets ¥100,000, and this tax year has no reduction for exceeding ¥10M in revenue two years prior. The ¥550,000 tier, and the ¥650,000 tier when reached through item 1, apply only if the return states that you are claiming the deduction and how it's computed, attaches a balance sheet, profit & loss statement, etc. prepared from your books, and is filed by the deadline (Sochiho Art. 25-2(6)). For the ¥650,000 tier reached through item 2, item 2 itself already requires sending the balance sheet, P/L, etc. via e-Tax by the deadline. If you elect the cash-basis method, neither the ¥650,000 nor ¥750,000 tier is available (¥100,000 still is).
>
> When there's real estate income, the blue-return special deduction is allocated from a single pool shared with business income (real estate is deducted first, then the remainder from business income). Real estate income that isn't a business-scale rental can't reach the higher tiers on its own (capped at ¥100,000), but if business income alone qualifies for a higher tier, that pool extends to the real estate portion too.

## 4. The home-worker necessary-expense special provision (Sochiho Art. 27)

For home workers, door-to-door sales agents, bill collectors, and similar people who continuously provide personal services to a specific party as the nature of their work, this provision raises the necessary-expense floor to ¥690,000 (¥650,000 for the 2025 tax year and earlier) when actual expenses fall short (effective 2026-12-01, applying from the 2026 tax year on). Choose it with the checkbox in § 2.

- **Cap on the floor**: the year's total revenue (business income plus miscellaneous income, excluding public pension). If there's salary income, the cap is ¥690,000 minus the salary income deduction
- **When both business and miscellaneous income exist**: under Enforcement Order Art. 18-2(2)(2)(イ)(ロ), the business side keeps its actual necessary expenses as-is (capped at its total revenue), and the remainder of the floor after subtracting the business side is allocated to the miscellaneous side (capped at its total revenue). Any part exceeding the miscellaneous side's total revenue reverts to the business side (also capped at its own total revenue)
- **Relationship with family-employee salary/deduction**: Income Tax Act Art. 57 falls within the 1st–5th categories that Sochiho Art. 27 replaces, so the "actual necessary expenses" compared against the floor includes blue-return family-employee salary (already an expense on the books) and the white-return family-employee deduction (deemed a necessary expense under Art. 57(3)). When the provision applies, business income = total revenue minus post-provision expenses, and the family-employee salary/deduction isn't subtracted again (see [10. `.xtx` export](10-xtx-export_en.md)). The family employee's own status (exclusion from being a co-residing spouse/dependent) is judged on the facts and doesn't change based on whether this provision applies
- Applies to both blue and white returns alike. The "pre-deduction business income" that the blue-return special deduction is based on is the post-provision amount (§ 3)

## 5. Reflecting this in the `.xtx` export

When generating the tax return `.xtx` in [10. `.xtx` export](10-xtx-export_en.md), if you've saved input on this screen, the income deduction/tax credit fields are automatically included. If nothing is saved (not entered), they're omitted as before, and you complete them yourself in e-Tax. If real estate income is entered, the real-estate financial statements (blue: KOA220, white: KOA130) are attached alongside the tax return (KOA020) in the same submission data.
